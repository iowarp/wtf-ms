# Literature Review

## Research Prompt
Which combinations of LPBF process parameters (laser power, scan speed, hatch spacing, layer thickness, and derived volumetric energy density) minimize porosity and lack-of-fusion defect density in Ti-6Al-4V, and what defect-based processing map can be extracted by synthesizing published process-defect datasets?

## Current State of Knowledge

### Defect formation mechanisms (keyhole porosity, lack-of-fusion, gas porosity)
The physical mechanisms behind the two dominant LPBF defect classes are well established. Lack-of-fusion (LOF) defects arise from insufficient energy input/penetration — the melt pool fails to fully remelt the previous layer or adjacent scan tracks, leaving irregular, crack-like unmelted-powder voids that are strongly detrimental to fatigue life ("Effects of Process Parameters and Process Defects on the Flexural Fatigue Life of Ti-6Al-4V Fabricated by Laser Powder Bed Fusion"). Keyhole porosity arises from excessive energy input: high recoil pressure from vaporization drives a deep, narrow vapor cavity whose instability traps gas bubbles that solidify as spherical pores ("Mechanism of keyhole pore formation in metal additive manufacturing," *npj Computational Materials*, 2022; "Keyhole fluctuation and pore formation mechanisms during laser powder bed fusion additive manufacturing," 2022). In-situ synchrotron X-ray imaging has directly resolved keyhole initiation, fluctuation (2.5–10 kHz), and collapse dynamics, giving a mechanistic (not just empirical) basis for defect-regime boundaries. Between these two failure modes lies a mid-parameter "optimal window" producing near-fully-dense material — a three-regime picture (LOF / dense / keyhole) that recurs across nearly every processing-map study reviewed.

### Volumetric energy density (VED) as an organizing metric — and its known limitations
VED = P/(v·h·t) (laser power / scan speed / hatch spacing / layer thickness) is the dominant single-number metric used to collapse the 4D parameter space and compare studies. Multiple sources report an empirically observed low-porosity VED band roughly in the 40–80 J/mm³ range for standard ~30 µm powder / ~30–60 µm layers, with one study reporting an optimum of 60–75 J/mm³ and porosity below 0.1% at specific optimized parameter triplets (e.g., 302.5 W / 990 mm/s / 0.14 mm). However, a substantial and growing critical literature documents that VED is **not a unique or sufficient predictor**: parameter sets sharing identical VED can produce systematically different pore type and density, VED conflates two physically distinct failure mechanisms (LOF vs. keyhole) into one scalar, and it does not represent melt-pool physics (non-linear parameter contributions, layer remelting, absorptivity) ("On the limitations of Volumetric Energy Density as a design parameter for Selective Laser Melting"; "Assessing Volumetric Energy Density as a Predictor of Defects in Laser Powder Bed Fusion 316L Stainless Steel," *JOM*, 2024). Modified/refined VED formulations (accounting for beam diameter, absorptivity, or melt-pool geometry) show improved correlation but are not yet standardized across the Ti-6Al-4V literature.

### Dimensionless/physics-based process maps as an alternative to VED
An alternative, more physically grounded framework uses dimensionless numbers derived from Eagar-Tsai-type thermal models — normalized enthalpy and the ratio of dwell time to thermal diffusion time (Rubenchik et al.) — to collapse melt-pool depth data across *different machines and materials* onto a single curve, in principle enabling rescaling of optimal parameters across platforms. Related scaling laws (Fabbro; Ye et al.; Hann et al.'s normalized enthalpy) are increasingly used to define universal keyhole-onset boundaries. This body of work is more mechanistic than VED-only processing maps but has been validated mainly on melt-pool geometry/keyholing, less thoroughly on LOF and on Ti-6Al-4V specifically across multiple independent datasets.

### Published process-defect datasets and data availability
Several structured, reusable datasets exist that are directly suited to literature/data synthesis: a Zenodo-hosted "Processing, microstructure, mechanical property dataset for laser powder bed fusion additively manufactured Ti-6Al-4V" spanning 42 parameter sets explicitly designed to probe dense/LOF/keyhole/balling regimes, reporting laser power, scan speed, hatch spacing, layer thickness, linear and modified VED, Archimedes density, and CT-derived pore size/shape/porosity; and a companion PMC-indexed "Dataset of process-structure-property feature relationship for laser powder bed fusion additive manufactured Ti-6Al-4V material." NIST's AM-Bench program and related NIST publications on transferability of data-driven AM knowledge are relevant infrastructure but are less centrally focused on a compiled porosity/LOF processing map for Ti-6Al-4V specifically than the Zenodo/PMC datasets identified above — this should be verified further during data acquisition rather than assumed.

### Cross-machine/platform generalizability
A recurring, explicitly stated challenge across sources is that nominally identical process parameters (P, v, h, t) do not reproduce identical density/defect outcomes across different LPBF OEM platforms (EOS, SLM Solutions/Nikon SLM, Renishaw, DMG Mori, Aconity, etc.) because of differences in laser beam diameter/profile, spot size, chamber atmosphere/gas flow, and powder feedstock characteristics. This is acknowledged as an open reproducibility problem rather than solved; the dimensionless-number approaches above are the most credible attempt at a machine-agnostic processing map but remain under-validated specifically for porosity/LOF prediction (as opposed to melt-pool depth) in Ti-6Al-4V.

### Layer thickness, productivity, and defect trade-offs
A distinct and increasingly active sub-topic (motivated by industrial throughput) examines whether layer thickness can be increased substantially (up to 100–250 µm, vs. conventional ~30–60 µm) while maintaining density above 99.9%. Multiple 2023–2025 studies report that thick-layer LPBF is achievable with re-optimized power/speed/hatch combinations and can raise build rate roughly 3–10x, but thicker layers also produce deeper keyholes at a given power (increasing keyhole-pore risk) and increased spatter/powder degradation. This sub-topic is a natural extension of the VED framework but is comparatively data-sparse and not yet consistently folded into standard VED-based processing maps.

## Research Gaps

### Gap 1: No cross-study, cross-machine-platform synthesized processing map exists for Ti-6Al-4V LOF/porosity — DIRECT
**Description**: Individual studies report VED-porosity relationships for a single machine/powder lot, but no identified publication systematically compiles (P, v, h, t, VED, porosity/LOF outcome) tuples across multiple independent studies and machine platforms into one validated processing map with explicit regime boundaries.
**Evidence**: Multiple sources explicitly note limited transferability/reproducibility across OEM platforms (beam diameter, atmosphere, powder differences) as an open, unresolved problem; individual reusable datasets (e.g., the Zenodo 42-parameter-set dataset) exist as single-source contributions rather than syntheses.
**Relevance**: This is exactly the core deliverable of the research prompt — a meta-analytic, multi-source processing map is the direct gap this project is scoped to fill.

### Gap 2: VED alone is demonstrated to be non-unique/insufficient for defect prediction, but no widely adopted multi-parameter alternative has been validated specifically for Ti-6Al-4V porosity/LOF across independent datasets — DIRECT
**Description**: The literature establishes clearly (mostly on 316L and IN718) that VED fails to uniquely predict defect outcomes, and proposes dimensionless-number alternatives (normalized enthalpy, refined VED) as more physically grounded, but these have not been cross-validated on a compiled multi-study Ti-6Al-4V porosity/LOF dataset.
**Evidence**: "On the limitations of Volumetric Energy Density..."; "Assessing Volumetric Energy Density as a Predictor of Defects in LPBF 316L Stainless Steel" (2024) — both explicitly state the non-uniqueness problem and call for improved metrics, but neither is a Ti-6Al-4V-specific multi-source validation.
**Relevance**: Directly addressable by this research — the planned statistical synthesis could test whether a refined/multi-parameter metric outperforms scalar VED on the compiled Ti-6Al-4V dataset, strengthening the processing-map output beyond a simple VED plot.

### Gap 3: Layer-thickness / high-productivity parameter regimes are comparatively data-sparse and not yet integrated into standard VED-based Ti-6Al-4V processing maps — ADJACENT
**Description**: Most classic Ti-6Al-4V processing maps assume conventional layer thickness (~30–60 µm); the emerging thick-layer (100–250 µm) productivity literature is smaller, more recent (2023–2025), and uses different parameter ranges that may not map cleanly onto conventional VED bands.
**Evidence**: Springer/MDPI 2023–2026 papers on thick-layer LPBF Ti-6Al-4V report re-optimized parameter sets and note deeper-keyhole risk at thicker layers, but this sub-literature is smaller and less standardized than conventional-layer-thickness studies.
**Relevance**: Adjacent to the core prompt (layer thickness is explicitly in scope) — the synthesis could flag whether thick-layer data points are consistent with or deviate from the conventional-layer VED processing map, which would itself be a useful finding, but this is not the central deliverable.

### Gap 4: Pore location/morphology (surface-connected vs. interior, near-contour vs. bulk) as a function of process parameters is inconsistently reported and rarely synthesized — ADJACENT
**Description**: While bulk porosity fraction and mean pore size are commonly reported, spatial distribution and morphology (aspect ratio, surface proximity) as a function of parameter set are less consistently extracted and compared across studies, despite morphology being flagged repeatedly as more mechanically relevant than porosity fraction alone.
**Evidence**: Multiple sources report average pore size/shape but with heterogeneous CT resolution and reporting conventions across studies, complicating direct meta-analytic comparison.
**Relevance**: In scope (morphology and location are named in RESEARCH.md scope) but likely a secondary rather than primary analysis given heterogeneous reporting — worth attempting but flagged as harder to synthesize than density-only measures.

### Gap 5: Mechanistic keyhole/LOF onset physics is well characterized via in-situ synchrotron studies, but rarely translated into simple, literature-extractable decision rules usable without specialized facility data — OUT-OF-SCOPE (context only)
**Description**: The most mechanistically rigorous keyhole/pore-formation studies rely on synchrotron X-ray or high-speed thermal imaging not available to literature-only researchers, creating a gap between mechanistic understanding and practically extractable empirical design rules.
**Evidence**: "Keyhole fluctuation and pore formation mechanisms..." and *Science* 2022 ML/keyhole-detection paper both rely on specialized in-situ facilities.
**Relevance**: Explicitly out of scope for this project (no lab/synchrotron access) — noted for completeness only; this research should use these papers for mechanistic framing but not attempt to replicate or extend their in-situ methodology.

## Loop-Back Assessment
PROMPT IS WELL-POSITIONED. The literature confirms this is an active, unresolved area: individual VED-porosity studies and mechanistic keyhole/LOF papers are abundant, but a synthesized, cross-study, multi-parameter processing map specifically for Ti-6Al-4V (Gap 1) and a validated alternative to scalar VED tested across independent datasets (Gap 2) are not present in the reviewed literature. The prompt is neither already-answered (no single paper or small paper set performs this cross-study synthesis) nor too broad (it is scoped to one alloy, one process, two defect types, and explicit sub-parameters). Feasibility is good: at least two structured, reusable datasets (Zenodo Ti-6Al-4V process-structure-property dataset; PMC companion dataset) were identified as strong candidate primary sources for the planned literature-scale statistical synthesis, and the VED-limitation literature (mostly published on 316L/IN718) gives a ready-made methodological template to adapt to Ti-6Al-4V.

## Review Notes
- The VED-limitations critique literature is richest for 316L stainless steel and IN718, not Ti-6Al-4V specifically. This research should explicitly note when methodological precedent is being transferred from another alloy system rather than assume it has been proven for Ti-6Al-4V.
- NIST AM-Bench was searched for explicitly but did not surface a clearly Ti-6Al-4V-porosity-specific dataset as directly as the Zenodo/PMC dataset did; its relevance to this specific project should be re-checked during the data-acquisition task rather than assumed from this review alone.
- The Rubenchik et al. dimensionless-scaling citation could not be fully bibliographically verified in this search pass (title/venue/exact year not independently confirmed) — it is flagged as unverified in the Key Papers table and should be confirmed (or dropped) before being cited in any final report.
- Cross-machine reproducibility is repeatedly described qualitatively ("known challenge") rather than quantified in any single paper found; the planned meta-analysis may itself be one of the first attempts to quantify this using literature-compiled data, which is a promising sign for novelty but means there is no existing benchmark to validate the synthesis methodology against.
